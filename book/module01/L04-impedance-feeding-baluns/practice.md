## Problem 1 — Radiation resistance and efficiency

A short dipole has length $\ell = 0.08\lambda$. Its conductors contribute a
loss resistance of $R_\text{loss} = 1.5\ \Omega$.

**(a)** Compute the radiation resistance
$R_\text{rad} = 80\pi^2 (\ell/\lambda)^2$.

**(b)** Compute the radiation efficiency $\eta_\text{rad}$.

**(c)** A short dipole has directivity $D = 1.5$ (1.76 dBi). What is its gain in
dBi?

## Problem 2 — Feeding a half-wave dipole with 50 Ω coax

**(a)** A dipole trimmed to resonance presents $Z_\text{in} = 70 + j0\ \Omega$.
Compute $\Gamma$, VSWR, return loss, and mismatch loss on a $50\ \Omega$ line.

**(b)** An *untrimmed* half-wave dipole presents $Z_\text{in} = 73 + j42.5\ \Omega$.
Compute $|\Gamma|$ and VSWR on the same line.

**(c)** Compare (a) and (b): in one sentence, what did trimming to resonance buy
you, and why?

## Problem 3 — Quarter-wave transformer

A resonant antenna presents a real input impedance of $R_L = 36\ \Omega$. You
want to match it to a $50\ \Omega$ feed line with a quarter-wave transformer.

**(a)** What characteristic impedance $Z_1$ must the $\lambda/4$ section have?

**(b)** What is $\Gamma$ at the design frequency?

**(c)** In one sentence, what happens to the match as you move off the design
frequency, and why?

## Problem 4 — VSWR, reflection, and the impedance behind it

An antenna measures **VSWR = 1.5** on a $50\ \Omega$ line.

**(a)** Find $|\Gamma|$.

**(b)** Find the return loss in dB.

**(c)** Find the mismatch loss in dB.

**(d)** Assuming the antenna is purely resistive, what two real input impedances
$Z_\text{in}$ are consistent with this VSWR?

## Problem 5 — Baluns

**(a)** In one or two sentences, explain why feeding a dipole *directly* with
coax (no balun) distorts the pattern.

**(b)** You need to feed a **folded dipole** ($Z_\text{in} \approx 292\ \Omega$)
with **$75\ \Omega$** coax. Which balun would you choose, and what does it do?

**(c)** You feed a **straight resonant dipole** ($\approx 70\ \Omega$) with
**$50\ \Omega$** coax. Which balun would you choose, and why is it a *different*
choice than (b)?
