# L29 and L30 lesson halves: the right depth (2026-11-17)

Neil, on the reshaped pages: "MTI processing isn't described anywhere in its
lesson, but CFAR is in incredible depth. Both feel like they miss the mark."
And the rule: **"give them enough to know what to expect, but not enough to
be able to implement the algorithms on their own."**

The course provides `radar_lab.py`; cadets run it, tune it and explain what
they see. The lesson half prepares them for that, and no further.

## The depth rule (MTI and CFAR, and any processing stage we provide)

Keep:
- What the stage is for, in one or two sentences, and where it sits in the
  chain (a small chain diagram with the stage marked).
- What it does to the picture the cadet will see: a before-and-after figure
  from the recorded data, early.
- The controls the lab and the app expose, and what turning each one does.
- What to expect at the kit: what should disappear, what should stay, what
  can go wrong and what that looks like.
- Exactly the relations Part 1 needs, each worked once on the teaching
  example (not the app's presets), per the reshape brief.

Cut:
- Code listings of the provided functions (`mti`, `cfar_2d`, and the like),
  and any step-by-step that amounts to an implementation recipe.
- Derivations and statistics the lab does not test.
- Edge cases, variants and design trade studies beyond what a control in the
  lab exposes.

Do not replace cut material with new material. Shorter is the point.
