Final Project — Radar Systems Report#

The final project is one report, in your own words, on the radar you built in Module 4: what the ADALM-PHASER hardware does, what each processing stage does to the signal, and what your own measurements show. It is an exploration of radar hardware and processing, written by someone who has run every stage of it on a real kit.

The labs are the scaffolding#

The L28–L31 Labs are the steps toward the report. Each one is a stage of a single radar processing chain on your own kit, and each one hands you the results, plots, and code that a section of the report is built on.

Each lab starts from the one before it: your L29 processing begins with your own L28 range code, the L30 Lab runs CFAR on your L29 maps, and the L31 Lab classifies what your L30 detector found. Keep your code, data, and plots from every lab.

The lab packets are not the report#

Turn in each lab packet as you finish the lab; it is checked and returned with feedback so that problems surface while there is time to fix them. But a stack of completed packets is not a final project. The packets answer the questions the packet asks; the report explains the radar. Text and answers lifted from your packets earn nothing in the report. What earns credit is your explanation of why the results came out as they did, tied together into one account of the system.

When things are due#

The lab packets come due one at a time, starting at class period 32, in the order you run the labs; each packet gives its own date. The report is due at class period 41, the last class of the term. Period numbers are the course calendar’s class meetings, not the lesson numbers on this site.

What is graded#

The report is 30% of your course grade, graded on a rubric with a sliding scale. The rubric is published with the L28 Lab packet, so read it before you start, not after you finish. It rewards understanding over results: a range resolution you can explain earns more than one you merely report.

Extra credit#

Do something with the radar that the labs did not ask for, and do it well: a new kind of target, a processing stage of your own in Python, a second moving target, a measurement the labs left out. Propose it to me in one paragraph and get it approved before you start. Extra credit is earned by the quality of the work, written up as its own section of the report.

Individual work#

The final project is individual: your own kit, your own code, and your own report. Collaboration follows the syllabus policy: discuss freely, but you must be able to explain every step you submit.
