    Module 4 — Radar Fundamentals and FMCW#

    ECE 444 · Module 04

    Radar Fundamentals and FMCW

    Send it, hear it come back, and work out what is out there.

    Lessons 25–31 · Antennas, Phased Arrays, and Radar Systems · Dr. Neil Rogers

    Where This Module Goes

    Radar is the antenna course with a clock attached. The radar equation says whether you will hear the echo at all; range, resolution and Doppler say what you can tell about the thing that sent it back.

    Then it gets real: FMCW on the PHASER, processed into range, a range waterfall, range-Doppler, MTI, and CFAR — the same chain a fielded radar runs. You build that chain one lab at a time, and then explain it in the final project, a report in your own words.

    Detection is a decision under noise, not a measurement. Probability of detection and false-alarm rate move together, and CFAR exists because the noise floor will not hold still. The module then ends on what a radar does with a detection once it has one: classify it from its micro-Doppler signature, and track it in range and angle.

    Learning Objectives 4.1-4.3

    1. I can apply the radar equation to calculate received power for a given geometry, and account for path loss and radar cross section (RCS).
    2. I can calculate range resolution, unambiguous range, and Doppler shift for a given radar waveform.
    3. I can apply radar detection theory (PD, FAR, dwell time) to determine detection performance under noise.

    Learning Objectives 4.4-4.6

    1. I can describe FMCW radar operation and configure an FMCW waveform on the ADALM-PHASER.
    2. I can process FMCW radar data to produce range, range-waterfall, and range-Doppler results.
    3. I can implement moving target indication (MTI) processing to distinguish moving targets from clutter.

    Learning Objectives 4.7-4.9

    1. I can apply constant false-alarm rate (CFAR) processing to radar data and evaluate detection performance.
    2. I can integrate FMCW radar processing with a phased-array front-end to track a moving target.
    3. I can classify a detected target from its micro-Doppler signature and report the classifier's accuracy.
