\question \textbf{The receive chain.} A wave from the HB100 reaches the
PHASER's patches and ends up as numbers in the Raspberry Pi.

\begin{parts}

	\part Put these six blocks in the order the signal meets them, from one
	patch to the Pi, and say in a few words what each one does: ADALM-Pluto,
	ADAR1000, frequency converter, LNA, patch, Raspberry Pi.

	\begin{center}
	\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.15}
	\begin{tabular}{| c | K{1.35in} | K{3.4in} |}
	\hline
	\textbf{Order} & \textbf{Block} & \textbf{What it does} \\\hline\hline
	1 & \kv{patch} & \kv{receives the wave; eight of them, $14$ mm apart} \\\hline
	2 & \kv{LNA} & \kv{amplifies each element before any loss} \\\hline
	3 & \kv{ADAR1000} & \kv{sets each element's phase and gain, sums four} \\\hline
	4 & \kv{frequency converter} & \kv{shifts each sum from X-band down to $2.2\ \ghz$} \\\hline
	5 & \kv{ADALM-Pluto} & \kv{digitizes the two channels} \\\hline
	6 & \kv{Raspberry Pi} & \kv{commands the chips, reads the samples, serves the GUI} \\\hline
	\end{tabular}
	\end{center}

	\begin{solution}
	The LNA comes first because the noise each later stage adds counts at the
	input divided by the gain ahead of it, and the phase shifters and the
	summing network are lossy. The frequency converter is there because the
	Pluto tunes no higher than $6\ \ghz$ and cannot digitize the $10\ \ghz$
	signal directly.
	\end{solution}

	\begin{minipage}[t]{0.58\linewidth}
		\part The ADAR1000 sets each element's phase with a 7-bit phase shifter.
		How many phase settings does it have, and how far apart are neighboring
		settings in degrees?
		\begin{solution}[0.9in]
		\eq{ 2^{7} &= 128 \text{ settings} \\
		     \frac{360\mydeg}{128} &= 2.8125\mydeg }
		\end{solution}
	\end{minipage}\hfill%
	\begin{minipage}[t]{0.37\linewidth}
		\raggedleft \vspace{12pt}
		settings = \ansbox[1.1in]{$128$}\\[10pt]
		step = \ansbox[1.1in]{$2.8125\mydeg$}
	\end{minipage}

	\begin{minipage}[t]{0.58\linewidth}
		\part How many elements feed one ADAR1000? How many signals leave the pair
		of ADAR1000s after their sums, and so how many channels does the Pluto
		digitize?
		\begin{solution}[0.9in]
		Each ADAR1000 takes four of the eight elements and sums them into one
		output, so two signals leave the pair, one per 4-element subarray, and
		the Pluto digitizes two channels.
		\end{solution}
	\end{minipage}\hfill%
	\begin{minipage}[t]{0.37\linewidth}
		\raggedleft \vspace{12pt}
		elements per chip = \ansbox[0.9in]{$4$}\\[10pt]
		signals out = \ansbox[0.9in]{$2$}\\[10pt]
		channels = \ansbox[0.9in]{$2$}
	\end{minipage}

\end{parts}

\question \textbf{The labeled block diagram.} Sketch the receive chain from the
patches to the Raspberry Pi. Label every block with its name and with the
frequency of the signal at that point, mark where the analog sum happens, and
mark where the two digital channels begin.

\begin{solutionorbox}[3.3in]
Patches (eight, $d = 14$ mm apart, at the HB100 frequency, such as
$10.525\ \ghz$) $\rightarrow$ ADL8107 LNA behind each patch (same frequency)
$\rightarrow$ two ADAR1000 beamformers, four elements each, phase and gain per
element, \textbf{the analog sum happens here} (same frequency, two outputs)
$\rightarrow$ two LTC5548 frequency converters, which shift each sum down to
$2.2\ \ghz$ $\rightarrow$ ADALM-Pluto (AD9361), listening at $2.2\ \ghz$ and
sampling at 3 MSPS, \textbf{the two digital channels begin here}
$\rightarrow$ Raspberry Pi over USB, which runs the backend, commands the
ADAR1000s over SPI, and serves the browser interface.
\end{solutionorbox}

\question \textbf{Bench diagnosis.} Two other teams ask for help with Part B.

\begin{parts}

	\part The first team presses \textbf{Start} and sees no peak anywhere on the
	FFT tab. Give the first two checks you would make, in order, and say what
	each one rules out.
	\begin{solutionorlines}[1.5in]
	First, confirm that the HB100 is powered and aimed at the array from about
	a meter; this rules out having no signal to receive, the most common cause.
	Second, run \textbf{Find HB100} and compare its result with the Signal Freq
	the GUI shows; this rules out the board listening at the wrong frequency,
	which puts the tone outside the $3\ \mhz$ window the FFT tab displays. Only
	after both pass is it worth suspecting the cables or the array.
	\end{solutionorlines}

	\part The second team's FFT tab shows a peak $25$ dB above the floor, but on
	the Rectangular tab its sweep stands only $8$ dB above the sweep's floor,
	against about $23$ dB on a working kit, and Est. Angle reads $11\mydeg$ with
	the source at boresight. What would you check next, and why?
	\begin{solutionorlines}[1.6in]
	The FFT peak shows that the signal reaches the SDR at a normal level, so the
	fault is in how the eight elements add. An uncalibrated array, from a
	\textbf{Calibrate} that was skipped, interrupted, or run with the source off
	boresight, leaves each element with its own phase error, so the elements do
	not add in phase at broadside and the main lobe never reaches full height.
	Check that Phase Control reads zero, then put the source back at boresight,
	$1\ \m$ out, run \textbf{Calibrate}, wait for it to finish, and sweep again.
	\end{solutionorlines}

\end{parts}

\question \textbf{Written answers.} Answer each in three or four sentences.

\begin{parts}

	\part Why does the control software have to measure the HB100's frequency
	instead of assuming $10.525\ \ghz$?

	\begin{solutionorlines}[1.7in]
	The HB100's oscillator is a free-running dielectric resonator, not a locked
	synthesizer, so its frequency is set by the size of the resonator and
	drifts with temperature; units land anywhere from about $10.1$ to
	$10.7\ \ghz$. The board shifts the source down to $2.2\ \ghz$ by an amount
	it computes from Signal Freq, and the SDR shows only a $3\ \mhz$ window
	there, one two-hundredth of that spread, so a frequency that is wrong by
	more than $1.5\ \mhz$ moves the tone off the display. The steering phase
	each element needs also depends on the frequency, through $k = 2\pi f/c$.
	So \textbf{Find HB100} measures the source and the software uses that
	number everywhere.
	\end{solutionorlines}

	\part The array has eight elements, but software sees only two digital
	channels. Explain where the other six went, and name one thing this rules
	out.

	\begin{solutionorlines}[1.9in]
	Each ADAR1000 applies a phase and a gain to its four elements and then sums
	them into a single output, so the individual element signals stop existing
	before anything is digitized. Two subarray sums reach the Pluto, and
	software cannot recover the eight element signals from them. That rules out
	anything that needs per-element data after the fact, such as forming
	several independent beams from one set of samples, or an adaptive
	beamformer that nulls more than one interferer. The MVDR run in Lesson 28
	has exactly two digital degrees of freedom for this reason, and one of them
	holds the look direction.
	\end{solutionorlines}

\end{parts}
