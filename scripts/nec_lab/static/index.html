Both traces are normalized to their own peak before they are differenced — a simulated pattern is absolute dBi and a chamber cut is raw S21 through cables and fixture, so nothing else puts them on one axis. The difference is clamped at the plot floor, because a null one degree off its predicted angle differences to tens of dB and means almost nothing.

3D pattern

Drag to rotate, scroll to zoom.

The radius in each direction is the gain in that direction and the color is the same number, always in dBi. The radius scale changes the shape, not the pattern: linear power is the donut from the lesson, and a dB radius inflates everything far from the peak until a dipole looks nearly round — useful for reading sidelobes, misleading about beam shape. The wire is drawn to scale at the center, so the nulls along its axis are the ones you can see. This is one more RP card than a cut — NEC has always computed the sphere.

NEC input file from the form

Hover any field to see what it means.

These cards are the actual interface to NEC, and they are identical in 4nec2, xnec2c and this tool. Edit them here and press Run these cards, or copy them into 4nec2. nec_lab reads CM CE GW GE GN EX FR RP XQ EN — any number of wires, any number of sources, and a perfect or real ground plane. Anything else — a load, a transmission line, an arc — is refused by name, because the rules, the energy audit and the studies on this page all assume the structure is what those cards describe.
