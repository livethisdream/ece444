    L22–L23 Lab — Tapering, Squint, and Quantization#

    ECE 444 · Fall 2026

    Tapering, Squint, and Quantization

    Taper the row, thin it, set its phases off frequency, and drop phase bits.

    L22–L23 Lab · Antennas, Phased Arrays, and Radar Systems · Dr. Neil Rogers

    Printing

    To print the slides, switch to present mode and print: one slide a sheet, with room for notes. Print from read mode for the whole lesson.

    Learning Objectives

    1. I can apply amplitude tapering (uniform, cosine, Chebyshev, Taylor) to control sidelobe level and predict the pattern trade-off.
    2. I can identify grating lobes, beam squint, and quantization effects in a phased array and describe their impact on system performance.

    Lesson 22 showed how an amplitude taper trades beamwidth and directivity for lower sidelobes, and why the peak drop the sweep plots is not the directivity the array loses. Lesson 23 showed three departures a taper cannot touch, because they come from the element spacing, the frequency the phases are set at, and the phase shifter’s resolution. Today you test both on your kit in four runs. Steps 1 to 6 bring up the kit with the HB100 at boresight. The taper runs fill Tables A and B in Part 2 of the packet, the grating-lobe run Table C, the squint run Table D, and the quantization run Table E, and you check every reading against the prediction you made in Part 1.

    The HB100 stays at boresight through Step 16. It moves to 45° for the squint run, and you sweep it in azimuth for the quantization run. Freeze the uniform trace in Step 7 and keep it on the plot through Step 16, because every peak drop is read against it.

    Predict First

    Before each run, predict its trace, then check yourself. Choose a run and a setting from the table above and decide, before you press Show trace: how wide the main lobe is next to the dashed reference and how far its peak falls, whether a sidelobe clears the floor, where the full-height lobes stand, how far the squinted peak moves from the source, and which bit counts raise a lobe. Then show it. Any change hides the trace again, so each guess comes first. The widget draws the ideal array factor at 10.0 GHz, not at your kit’s frequency, so its numbers are close to your Part 1 values but not equal to them, and it leaves out the peak drops Part 1 asks for. The floor is an example at 23 dB down; you read your own in Step 8.

    Bring Up the Kit

    • HB100 at boresight, 1.2 m out, at patch-row height; keep ±40° clear.

    • Find HB100, Calibrate, then set Rx Gain for a −10 dBFS peak.

    • Load the Tapering preset with Enforce Symmetric Taper on.

    Place the HB100 at boresight, 1.2 m from the array face, at patch-row height and aimed at the center of the patch row. Check the 1.2 m against the far-field distance you predicted in the packet’s setup table: the sweep reads the far-field pattern only beyond it. Every taper number is a comparison between sweeps, so the source stays put until Step 17. Keep ±40° either side of the kit clear of walls and people, because a reflection off either raises a false lobe on the sweep.

    1. Place the source. Put the HB100 at boresight, 1.2 m out, at patch-row height, and record the distance.

    2. Connect. Browse to your kit’s address and wait for the pill to read Connected.

    3. Find the source. Press Find HB100 and record the Signal Freq it reports. If it differs by more than about 1% (100 MHz) from the frequency you used in Part 1, rework your predictions before you sweep.

    4. Calibrate the array. Press Calibrate and wait for it to finish, with the HB100 still at boresight; it uses the frequency Find HB100 stored. An uncalibrated array carries element-to-element gain and phase errors that look exactly like a taper you did not ask for. If the uniform trace’s two first sidelobes differ by more than about 2 dB in Step 8, calibrate again.

    5. Set the gain. Set Rx Gain so the FFT peak, beam at 0° and source at boresight, reads \(-10 \pm 3\) dBFS, and record it. Leave it there for the whole lab, so every peak compares.

    6. Load the preset. Open Lab Presets and choose the Tapering preset; it loads the HB100 configuration and opens the Rectangular tab. Under Element Gains, check that Enforce Symmetric Taper is on, so each slider moves its mirror-image partner.

    Taper Runs

    • Freeze a uniform sweep: every peak drop is read against it.

    • Sweep Hann, then Blackman and Chebyshev if time allows.

    • Read levels in dBc, and record the noise floor.

    • Design a taper to the Step 12 specification.

    Read every level in dBc: subtract the trace’s own peak from the reading, so the peak is 0 dBc. The plot reads dBFS, and a tapered trace peaks lower than the uniform one, so only a level taken from its own trace’s peak means the same thing on every trace. The one exception is the peak drop, which you read against the frozen uniform trace.

    1. Freeze the reference. Choose the Uniform taper, check that all eight sliders read 100%, press Start, and read Peak Gain and Angle (set to Peak). Press Freeze: this trace stays on the plot as the reference every peak drop is read against.

    2. Read the uniform trace. Record the HPBW, the angles where the trace falls 3 dB below its own peak, the first sidelobe, and the noise floor. Compare the HPBW with your Part 1 value: the sweep steps one 2.8125° phase step a point, about a degree of angle near broadside, so the 3 dB crossings land between samples and a degree of difference is within the reading. If the two first sidelobes, near ±22°, differ by more than about 2 dB, calibrate again and repeat Steps 7 and 8.

    3. Load Hann. Choose the Hann taper and check the eight sliders read 12, 43, 77, 100, 100, 77, 43, 12. Check your Predicted cells for it in Table A.

    4. Sweep Hann. Press Start and record the HPBW, the peak drop against the frozen uniform trace, and the first sidelobe, and compare each with your prediction beside it in Table A. On a kit without the gain-slider fix, the slider is not linear in voltage, and every taper comes out milder than your prediction, its beam narrower and its peak drop smaller by about the same fraction on every taper. Record what you measure; Part 3 asks you to account for any difference.

    5. Blackman and Chebyshev (optional). Repeat Steps 9 and 10 for each, if time allows, and compare with your predictions.

    6. Design your own taper. With Enforce Symmetric Taper on, meet this specification: HPBW no wider than 16°, with the first sidelobe below −20 dBc. Start from the taper you designed in Part 1, the Predicted row of Table B. Set the sliders, sweep, read the HPBW and sidelobe, and adjust: raise the end elements to narrow the beam, and lower them if the sidelobe rises past −20 dBc. Record your final gains and the result.

    7. Restore. Choose the Uniform taper again, so the next runs start from a known state.

    Grating Lobes and Squint

    • Rx1, Rx4, Rx7 on, then Rx1 and Rx5: read each lobe’s angle and height.

    • Source to 45°: sweep at Signal BW 10 MHz, then 500 MHz.

    • Check Signal BW before every squint sweep.

    1. Check the reference. Leave the source at boresight with the Uniform taper loaded, and check that the frozen uniform trace from Step 7 is still on the plot. If it is not, press Start and Freeze again: every peak drop below is read against it.

    2. Every third element. Turn Enforce Symmetric Taper off, since the gains below are not symmetric, and choose the Grating Lobes preset. It leaves Rx1, Rx4, and Rx7 at 100% and the other five at 0%, so the active elements sit \(3d = 42\ \text{mm}\) apart. Press Start and record the lobe angles, their heights against the 0° lobe, and the peak drop against the frozen uniform trace. Compare each with your prediction.

    3. Every fourth element. Set Rx4 and Rx7 to 0% and Rx5 to 100%, so only Rx1 and Rx5 are on, \(4d = 56\ \text{mm}\) apart, and sweep again. Record the same three, and compare them with your prediction, including what it says about both ends of the plot. The measured peak drops in Steps 15 and 16 run about 1 dB short of the element count, even with elements fully on or off, for a reason that is still open, so I grade these two steps on the lobe angles.

    4. Squint reference. Place the HB100 at 45° on the 1.2 m arc and choose the Uniform taper. Set Signal BW to 10 MHz, press Start, record Angle (set to Peak; about 45°, the source), and press Freeze.

    5. Squint. Choose the Beam Squint preset, check that Signal BW reads 500 MHz, and sweep again. Record Angle and its shift from Step 17, and compare the shift with your Part 1 answer. The source has not moved; only the frequency the GUI computes the phases at has. Calibrate, Find HB100, and the Rect preset all reset Signal BW to 0, so check it before every squint sweep.

    Quantization and Restore

    • HB100 to the arc’s −90° end; the Quantization preset holds a Blackman beam at 15°.

    • Sweep the source in azimuth at 7, 4, 3, then 2 bits.

    • Record the strongest lobe outside the main lobe, then restore the kit.

    1. Load the preset. Put the HB100 at the arc’s −90° end and choose the Quantization preset. It holds the beam at 15° with the Blackman taper, so every true sidelobe sits under the noise floor and any lobe that rises comes from rounding, and it opens Signal vs Time on the Tracking tab.

    2. Sweep at 7 bits. With Use Bits checked, set Phase Shift Bits to 7, then sweep the HB100 in azimuth from −90° to +90° along the 1.2 m arc, aimed at the array, in about 20 s. Record the strongest lobe outside the main lobe, in dBc, and where it is; write “floor” where nothing rises out of it. Signal vs Time records 30 s from when the mode starts, so begin the sweep promptly; a slower sweep runs off its end.

    3. Sweep at 4, 3, and 2 bits. Repeat Step 20 at each, checking the bit count and the Use Bits box before each sweep, and compare each trace with your Part 1 prediction.

    4. Restore. Before you leave: Use Bits on, Phase Shift Bits 7, Signal BW 10 MHz, and the Uniform taper.

    No Hardware?

Steps 17 to 21 need a source that moves, so they have no simulator equivalent; use your Part 1 predictions in their place.

    Lab Packet

    The L22–L23 Lab packet is one submission for the whole lab, turned in complete before Lesson 24. It has three parts:

    1. Part 1, Predict (individual, before the lab period, because you need it in class): the Predicted cells, shaded gray, in every table, at your kit’s frequency, with your work for one row of each table attached on your own paper.

    2. Part 2, Measure (your own kit’s readings, recorded in class): the Measured cells beside your predictions, for the four presets, your own taper, two thinned rows, the squint pair, and four quantization sweeps.

    3. Part 3, Analyze (individual, after the lab): three open prompts, each answered from your own readings.

    • L22–L23 Lab packet (PDF)

    For Part 3

    Part 3 is done after class with your Part 2 entries and your Part 1 predictions beside you. It asks about three comparisons:

    1. The tapers: compare your taper measurements (Tables A and B) with your predictions, and choose a taper for this kit.

    2. Grating lobes and squint: compare your grating-lobe measurement (Table C) and your beam-squint measurement (Table D) with your predictions.

    3. Phase bits: compare your four quantization sweeps (Table E) with each other and with your predictions.

    I grade each answer on a claim, the evidence from your own numbers, the reasoning that connects them, and the uncertainty. Lesson 22’s ”Reading a Tapered Sweep” says how to read each kind of level against its reference.
