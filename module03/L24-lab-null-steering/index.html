    Null Steering

    A computed notch, a structural null, an adaptive null, and the monopulse pair, measured on your own kit.

    L24 Lab · Antennas, Phased Arrays, and Radar Systems · Dr. Neil Rogers

    Tracking preset, Mode

    Lesson 24 built four nulls, and today you measure each one on your kit. In Procedure A you load the gain and phase table you computed in Part 1 and sweep it. In Procedure B you subtract the two digital channels with Beam 1 Phase. In Procedure C the Phaser software adds a simulated interferer, first at +20° and then at +30°, and you compare Manual with MVDR on a beam held at boresight. In Procedure D the Tracking preset forms the sum and delta beams together, and Tracking mode follows the HB100 as it moves. Step 6 freezes the uniform trace, and every level in Procedures A and B reads against its peak. Each block checks a Part 1 prediction: its readings fill one table in Part 2 of the packet, and you compare each with what you predicted.

    Predict First

    Before you sweep, predict each trace, then check yourself. Choose a setting from the list and decide, before you press Show, what the trace does next to the dashed uniform pattern: where its nulls and peaks sit, how far the main lobe drops, and, for the error trace, which side reads positive. Then show it. Choosing another setting hides the trace again, so each guess comes first. The widget draws the ideal array at 10.0 GHz, not at your kit’s frequency, so its numbers are close to your Part 1 values but not equal to them, and it has no room reflections or calibration residue, so its nulls go deeper than any kit’s.

    Bring Up the Kit

    • HB100 at boresight, 1.2 m out, at patch-row height.

    • Keep ±40° of the room clear of walls and people.

    • Find HB100, Calibrate, then set Rx Gain for a −10 dBFS peak.

    Place the HB100 at boresight, 1.2 m from the array face, at patch-row height and aimed at the center of the patch row. The notch and the difference null are deeper than anything the earlier labs measured, so anything that reflects the HB100’s tone in from another direction fills them: keep about ±40° on each side of boresight clear of walls and people for the whole lab. Lesson 24’s ”Measured Null Depth” gives the depth a cramped room allows.

    1. Browse to your kit’s address from Lesson 17 and wait for the pill to read Connected.

    2. With the HB100 at boresight, press Find HB100 and record the Signal Freq it reports and your source distance. Compare it with your Part 1 frequency: a change of 0.08 GHz moves the notch only about 0.2°, so keep your table.

    3. Press Calibrate and wait for it to finish, with the HB100 still at boresight. An uncalibrated array fills a null on its own, and a shallow notch would then measure the calibration rather than your weights.

    4. Set Rx Gain (dB) for an FFT peak of \(-10 \pm 3\) dBFS, beam at 0°, and record it. Leave it there for the whole lab.

    5. Check Signal BW. Calibrate, Find HB100, and every preset reset it to zero, so check it again before each sweep you record.

    Procedure A: The Static Notch

    • Sweep the uniform array and freeze it as the reference.

    • Type in your Part 1 gains and phases, each phase with its sign.

    • Sweep again, and read the peak and the notch’s depth and angle.

    1. With the array uniform (Element Gains at 100 %, Phase Control reset), press Start. Read Peak Gain and Angle (set to Peak), then press Freeze to hold the trace as your reference. Record the level of the sidelobe nearest +22.5° in dBc, its reading minus the uniform peak, so the frozen peak is 0 dB for every level in Procedures A and B. Compare it with your Table A prediction. If the two first sidelobes differ by more than about 2 dB, calibrate again and repeat this step.

    2. Enter your Part 1 gains into Element Gains (Rx1 through Rx8). Leave Enforce Symmetric Taper off: the set is already symmetric, and with the switch on a later edit to one slider is mirrored onto its partner.

    3. Enter your Part 1 phases into Phase Control, each with its sign, and check every sign before you sweep. Lesson 24’s ”The PHASER Settings” says where a sign error puts the notch, so look there too if Step 9 shows no notch where you expect it.

    4. Press Start and read the new trace against the frozen one: the new Peak Gain, the notch’s depth below the frozen peak, and the notch’s angle, and compare each with your Table A prediction. A notch much shallower than your prediction points to the room first: a wall or a person beside the kit, or a stale calibration.

    5. Press Reset in Phase Control and return Element Gains to Uniform.

    Procedure B: The Difference Null

    • Set Mode to Manual and Beam 1 Phase to 180°.

    • Sweep, and read the null and the two peaks beside it.

    • Set Beam 1 Phase back to 0°.

    1. Open Digital Beam Forming and set Mode to Manual.

    2. Leave Beam 0 Gain and Phase alone, and set Beam 1 Phase to 180°.

    3. Press Start. Nothing in the analog beamformers changed; only one digital channel’s sign did.

    4. Record the null’s depth below the frozen uniform peak and its angle, and the angles of the two peaks that replace the main lobe. The sweep steps the phase by one 2.8125° LSB, about 1° of angle here, so one step is the reading tolerance. Compare each reading with your Table B prediction.

    5. Set Beam 1 Phase back to 0°.

    Procedure C: MVDR Against an Interferer

    • Hold the beam on the HB100 at boresight in Signal vs Time.

    • Inject a simulated interferer at +20°; read Manual, then MVDR.

    • Read the held pattern on the Rectangular tab, then repeat at +30°.

    The interferer in this procedure is simulated: the Phaser software adds a second source, at an angle and level you choose, to the real samples the kit receives, before the digital beamformer sees them. The target stays your HB100 at boresight. In Signal vs Time the beam is held still. The Tracking tab plots the held beam’s level against time, the two readout tiles under the plot, Interferer vs Target and MVDR Suppression, give the numbers, and the Rectangular tab draws the held beam’s pattern against arrival angle: the conventional pattern dashed, the weighted pattern solid, and lines at the look direction and the interferer. Procedure C needs the current Phaser software on your kit.

    1. Set Mode to Signal vs Time with Steer Angle at 0°. This holds the beam on the target, and the readout tiles fill in only while it is held.

    2. Open the Interferer pane, below Digital Beam Forming, and press Inject Interferer; the plot shows an Interferer ON badge. Set Angle (deg) to +20 and Power (dBFS) to 0, its maximum, which puts it about 10 dB above a target read at −10 dBFS. Leave Offset from target (kHz) at 100 and Coherent with target off.

    3. In Digital Beam Forming, set Mode to Manual. In Manual the interferer only adds signal, and the Rectangular plot shows its line sitting on a sidelobe of the pattern. Watch ten updates of Interferer vs Target and Peak Gain and record the median of each, the middle value once sorted. The readings jump between updates, and the median ignores the odd wild one where an average would not. Compare Interferer vs Target with your Table C prediction.

    4. Set Mode to MVDR, Snapshots to 1024, and Diagonal Load to 0.1. Leave Steer Angle at 0°: it is MVDR’s look direction. MVDR has one null to place, because the board has two digital channels. It places it on the interferer while protecting the target it measures in the look direction, so the target must be where the beam points. Record the medians of Interferer vs Target, Peak Gain, and MVDR Suppression. The suppression tile needs a Manual and an MVDR reading at the same Steer Angle; if it shows “–”, repeat Step 18, then this step. A suppression well under 10 dB at +20° points to the beam first: check that Steer Angle is 0° and the HB100 still sits at boresight.

    5. Open the Rectangular tab. Record the angle and depth of the solid weighted pattern’s null near +20°, and the dashed conventional pattern’s level at the same angle. The weighted pattern changes elsewhere too; Lesson 24’s ”What MVDR Chooses” says what to look for.

    6. Move the interferer to +30°. Record Peak Gain in Manual, then in MVDR at Diagonal Load 0.001 and at 0.1, and watch Interferer vs Target. Compare the Peak Gains with your Table C prediction.

    7. Press the interferer button to turn it off, and set Mode back to Manual and Beam Sweep.

    Procedure D: Watch It Track

    • Choose the Tracking preset and sweep the sum, delta, and error traces.

    • Read the delta null, the error’s zero, and its sign on each side.

    • Choose Tracking mode, then move the HB100 and turn the kit.

    1. Open Lab Presets and choose the Tracking preset; its buttons respond only while the section is open. It turns on the delta and error traces, sets both digital beams to 0°, and leaves the beam sweeping. Check Signal BW again, which the preset resets.

    2. Check that the taper is Uniform under Element Gains, and that Digital Beam Forming reads Mode Manual with Beam 0 Phase and Beam 1 Phase at 0°. MVDR forms no delta beam, so the tracker holds still under it.

    3. On the Rectangular tab, press Start. The sum trace peaks on boresight and the delta trace nulls there. Record the delta null’s depth in dBc, below the sum trace’s peak, and its angle.

    4. Read the Error trace. It crosses zero once near boresight, where the beam points at the HB100. Record that angle, and the sign of the error on each side of it, and compare both with your Table D prediction.

    5. Choose Tracking from the Mode menu and open the Tracking tab, which plots the tracked steering angle against update number; Tracking mode needs the current Phaser software on your kit. The first update sweeps and locks on the HB100; after that each update holds the beam and steps it one phase LSB toward the side the error points, and the Rectangular plot marks where the beam sits. Move the HB100 slowly in azimuth along the 1.2 m arc and watch the tracked angle follow it. If the beam moves away from the source instead, the kit’s direction setting is reversed: stop and tell me. Then bring the HB100 back to boresight, turn the kit on its stand to 10° and 20° each way by the stand’s scale, and record the tracked angle at each, once it settles.

    No Hardware?

    Note

    Run the backend in simulation mode with python phaser_headless.py --sim, or use the hosted simulator, and open the same URL. The simulator places a target at boresight, so procedures A, B, and D run as written: the weights, the notch, the difference null, and the error function are all in its physics model. It also carries a simulated interferer, so procedure C runs there too.

    Lab Packet

    The L24 Lab packet is one submission for the whole lab, turned in complete before Lesson 25. It has three parts:

    1. Part 1, Predict (individual, before the lab period, because you need it in class): the gains and phases you type in for Procedure A, and the shaded Predicted cells of Tables A to D, at your kit’s frequency, with your work for one row of each table attached on your own paper.

    2. Part 2, Measure (your own kit’s readings, recorded in class): the white Measured cells of the same tables, one table per procedure, each beside its prediction.

    3. Part 3, Analyze (individual, after the lab): three open prompts, each answered from your own readings.

    • L24 Lab packet (PDF)

    For Part 3

    Part 3 is done after class with your Part 2 entries and your Part 1 predictions beside you. It asks about three comparisons:

    1. Three nulls: compare your notch (Table A), your difference null (Table B) and your delta null (Table D) with each other and with your predictions.

    2. MVDR at two angles: compare your Manual and MVDR readings, with the interferer at +20° and at +30° (Table C), with your predictions, and say which conclusions would hold for a real interferer.

    3. The tracker: compare your Table D readings, the beam sweep and the tracked angles, with your predictions and with the stand’s scale.

    I grade each answer on a claim, the evidence from your own numbers, the reasoning that connects them, and the uncertainty. Lesson 24’s ”Measured Null Depth”, ”Two Limits of Two Channels” and ”Closing the Loop” give the mechanisms. For alignment, Lesson 19’s ”The Bearing Error Budget” sizes the error of aligning a kit by eye.
